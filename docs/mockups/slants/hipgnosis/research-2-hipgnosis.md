# Hipgnosis, second round: process, deep cuts, craft, and UI translations

This brief extends `research.md` in the same folder. It does not repeat the covers, palette, type, or CSS recipes from there. Facts marked **(verified)** were checked against a source in section 6 this round. Facts marked **(from memory)** were not checked. Treat them as leads, not citations.

## 1. Process and thinking

### How a Hipgnosis cover got made

- **Roles.** Storm was the eccentric ideas man. Po was the practical partner who ran shoots and handled clients. Po took most of the photographs. (verified, Spectator review of a Po Powell book; Interview magazine)
- **Idea first, band second.** The studio sold a concept, not a portrait. When Genesis did not want to appear on *The Lamb*, Hipgnosis cast a model ("Omar") as Rael. *...And Then There Were Three...* also used models instead of the band. (verified, Wikipedia)
- **Several roughs, one quick yes.** Storm brought rough ideas to the band. For *Dark Side of the Moon*, the band picked one design in seconds and left the room. (verified, Q magazine 1992, Storm's account)
- **Shorthand for the music.** Storm: "All I try to do is represent the music." "I have to use shorthand." (verified, Creative Review)
- **Do it for real.** Storm: "my basic inclination has been to do it for real." "I don't fake it." "What I do is set someone on fire or build a statue and photograph it." (verified, Creative Review)
- **Photography because it is real.** Storm: "I like photography because it is a reality medium, unlike drawing which is unreal." And "I like to mess with reality ... to bend reality." (verified, Wikipedia) He also joked: "That's because I can't paint!" (verified, Seconds #46, 1998)
- **Puns from the title or lyric.** The method is literal. *Technical Ecstasy* became "mechanical copulation", so two robots meet on an escalator. *Force It* became a bathroom full of taps (UK "faucet"). *Never Say Die* and *Difficult to Cure* use masked figures. *Year of the Cat* puts a cat-obsessed woman in a mirror. (verified, Wikipedia and hypergallery)
- **No explanation offered.** Storm: "I like pictures that don't necessarily have an explanation off pat." (verified, Q 1992) And: "my imagery provokes inquiry but it's quite friendly." (verified, Seconds #46)
- **Landscapes as mind movies.** Storm: "I particularly like landscapes, which I use in conjunction with 'mind movies'." (verified, Q 1992)
- **Objects out of time.** On *Presence*, he wanted to "tamper with nostalgic pictures of the '30s and '40s with an object from the future." (verified, Q 1992) This is the single most useful line for a retrofuturist blend.
- **Packaging as a playground.** Storm: "Gatefold LPs were a place to exercise our magic." (verified, Seconds #46)
- **Budget forced cleverness.** Storm: "I have budgetary struggles... but it just means I have to box clever." (verified, Creative Review)
- **Recycled ideas.** Rejected ideas moved to other clients. The masked-surgeons idea was turned down by Black Sabbath for *Never Say Die* and became Rainbow's *Difficult to Cure* (1981). UFO's *Obsession* reused a ball-bearing job made for a Japanese magazine. Steve Hackett claims Genesis turned down the idea that became *Presence*; that claim is unconfirmed. (verified as claims, Wikipedia and Interview)
- **The end.** Hipgnosis dissolved in 1983. Storm and Po formed Greenback Films for music videos. Po saw punk coming: the *Never Mind the Bollocks* sleeve "must have cost about 2p", and he felt "the writing's on the wall for our style." (verified, Wikipedia and Spectator)

### *Squaring the Circle* (Anton Corbijn, 2022)

- A documentary on the studio. It was released in US cinemas in June 2023. (verified, Reason)
- Interviewees include Po, David Gilmour, Roger Waters, Nick Mason, Robert Plant, Jimmy Page, Peter Gabriel and Paul McCartney. Storm, who died in 2013, appears in archive footage. (verified for the first six; Gabriel and McCartney from memory)
- It is shot mostly in black and white, in Corbijn's style. (from memory)
- Corbijn on the prism: "It's so striking in its simplicity. I mean, children can love it." (verified, Interview)
- Po on the prism: "It's so simple and so un-Hipgnosis, because we were known for photo designs, not for graphics." (verified, Interview)
- Po on *Wish You Were Here*: "it was shot for real, which was important." (verified, Interview)
- Jimmy Page on *In Through the Out Door*: the cover was "better than the album itself." (verified, Reason)
- Studio lore: the first office had no toilet, and visitors were told "We pee in the sink." Syd Barrett wrote "Hipgnosis" in biro on the flat door, which gave the studio its name. (verified, Reason and Spectator)

## 3. Production craft

- **Hasselblad photographs, then a print workshop.** Most covers began as real photos. The finished art was a retouched print, not the negative. (verified, Manning)
- **Richard Manning, retoucher.** He describes the aim as "to improve the photograph": "softly bleaching dark areas and opening up what is already there." He worked "with a sable brush and photo dyes." On the burning man he matched the suit with "dark grey with touches of magenta and cyan." (verified, UCR)
- **How *Technical Ecstasy* was painted.** Manning started from a dye-transfer print of real escalators and Hardie's trace of the robots. He masked everything but the glass sides and bleached them white. He washed in a yellow ochre with wet cotton wool, then airbrushed shadows. The female robot was masked and sprayed in segments. The male was sprayed grey as one piece, then shaded. Repeated masking and bleaching weakened the print. (verified, Manning site) So the "machine" look is hand airbrush on photo, not illustration on blank paper.
- **Dye wash and airbrushed skies.** *Going for the One* used double-weight paper, a light blue dye wash, airbrushed clouds and photomontage. The coloured lines were added at the printing stage. (verified, Wikipedia)
- **Hand-tinting and montage.** *Silk Torpedo* was "two black-and-white shots montaged together" and hand-tinted. *Saucerful* was masked by hand in the darkroom. Po: "printing various different images and masking off with my hands." (verified, UCR, Interview)
- **George Hardie, graphics.** He drew the pen-and-ink logos and the "high-tech" line work: the prism, the Genesis Deco logo, the *Band on the Run* title, the *Technical Ecstasy* robots, and the *Going for the One* layout. (verified, Wikipedia)
- **Other hands.** Colin Elgie illustrated (*Trick of the Tail*, *Year of the Cat*). Geoff Halpin did graphics (*Quark*). (verified)
- **Staged for real.** Real fire, real water in a phone box, real footballs in the desert, a real sheep on a couch. When something went wrong, they reshot. (verified)
- **Type as the image.** *Go 2* is the clearest case: the cover is a block of text that explains itself. Hipgnosis graphics otherwise stayed small: logos, spines, stickers. (verified)

## 4. Motifs catalogue

One line each. Tech and futurism motifs are marked **[T]**.

- **[T] Machines in love.** Robots on an escalator (*Technical Ecstasy*).
- **[T] Robot as emblem.** A painted robot with an atom brain over a real airport (*I Robot*).
- **[T] Tubes and escalators.** Glass tubes and moving stairs as paths through time (*I Robot*, *Technical Ecstasy*).
- **[T] Power station.** Battersea, outside (*Animals*) and inside (*Quark*).
- **[T] Craft in the sky.** A spaceship over a gorge (*Argus*), a bulb as a spaceship (*ELO 2*).
- **[T] Object from the future in an old photo.** The black obelisk (*Presence*).
- **[T] Light trails.** Time-lapse traces as a record of movement (*...And Then There Were Three...*).
- **[T] Geometric overlay on a photo.** Coloured lines crossing a skyline (*Going for the One*).
- **[T] Masks and protective gear.** Pilots (*Never Say Die*), surgeons (*Difficult to Cure*).
- **[T] Aviation.** A vintage trainer cockpit (*Never Say Die*).
- **[T] Ball bearings.** Machined spheres (*Obsession*).
- **Lone figure from behind.** Facing a tower, a gorge, a desert (*Going for the One*, *Argus*, *WYWH*).
- **Storyboard grid.** Frames of one figure across a story (*The Lamb*).
- **Literal pun.** Taps for "Force It", gum for "Lovedrive".
- **Element in a box.** Water in a phone box (*Deadlines*).
- **Many of one thing in a landscape.** Footballs (*Elegy*), later beds (*Momentary Lapse*).
- **Animal out of place.** A sheep on a couch, a cow, a pig.
- **Mirror and costume.** Self seen in a reflection (*Year of the Cat*).
- **Self-describing text.** The cover explains it is a cover (*Go 2*).
- **Vandalised print.** Torn paper, smeared Polaroid, thrown tomatoes.
- **Deco and technical lettering.** Hardie's logos.

## 5. UI translations for Brain2

Each pattern names the source, the UI element, and the real data it carries. Items marked **(subtle)** can sit inside the existing retro hardware UI (beige/grey milled panels, CRT phosphor screens, pixel font) without replacing it.

1. **The Object on the timeline** (*Presence*). **Header / today marker.** A small black obelisk glyph sits on the day timeline at "now". It moves with the clock. Its height can show how much is left today. **(subtle)** It is one 4×12px black slab on a CRT strip.

2. **Escalator queue** (*Technical Ecstasy*, *I Robot*). **Progress / up-next list.** Tasks ride a diagonal escalator. The top step is "next". Steps advance one at a time as tasks finish. The step count shows how far down a task sits. Draw the steps as stacked 1px phosphor lines.

3. **Two robots meet** (*Technical Ecstasy*). **Toast for a linked or merged item.** When two tasks link (a dependency met, a duplicate merged), two small robot glyphs, one curved and one angular, slide together in the toast. Show the two task names under them. Keep it rare.

4. **Atom-brain robot** (*I Robot*). **Settings / automation rules.** The settings page for recurring rules gets a robot head icon. The atom's orbit count equals the number of active rules. **(subtle)** A pixel-font icon on a milled panel badge.

5. **Light trails** (*...And Then There Were Three...*). **Detail drawer history.** A task's history is a single long-exposure strip. Each status change leaves a light trace. Trace brightness is time spent in that state. On a CRT this is a phosphor afterglow line. **(subtle)**

6. **Time-lapse timer** (*...And Then There Were Three...*). **Focus timer.** The ring fills with a fading trail instead of a solid arc. Old seconds dim like phosphor persistence. The trail length is the elapsed time.

7. **Storyboard grid** (*The Lamb*). **Weekly review.** Seven monochrome frames, one per day. Each frame shows the day's top task and a done count. One frame is "stepped outside": the current day, raised and lit.

8. **Self-describing empty state** (*Go 2*). **Empty state.** Plain mono text: "This is an EMPTY LIST. This writing is the DESIGN upon the empty list. The DESIGN is to help you ADD a task." Show the real shortcut key and the list name inside the text. **(subtle)** It fits a CRT screen with no graphics at all.

9. **Spaceship rising** (*Argus*). **Upcoming / deadline indicator.** A small craft glyph rises over a low horizon as a due date nears. Height above the horizon is days left, inverted. At due, it is overhead.

10. **Bulb as spaceship** (*ELO 2*). **Idea inbox counter.** Captured ideas show as a small bulb icon with a count. The filament glows brighter as the inbox grows, as a nudge to triage.

11. **Power station chimneys** (*Animals*, *Quark*). **Capacity / load gauge.** Four chimneys are four areas or projects. Smoke height is the open task count. A chimney with no smoke is a quiet area. **(subtle)** Four bar meters with a chimney cap on a milled panel.

12. **Geometric lines over a photo** (*Going for the One*). **Filter overlay.** Each active filter draws a thin coloured line across the list header. Three filters, three lines. Click a line to remove that filter.

13. **Masked figures** (*Never Say Die*, *Difficult to Cure*). **Privacy / hidden mode.** Private tasks show a masked avatar slot and a redacted title until hovered. Count the masked items in the header.

14. **Element in a box** (*Deadlines*). **Overdue modal.** A boxed panel slowly "fills" from the bottom with a tint as an item stays overdue. The fill level is the days overdue, capped at a week.

15. **Many of one thing** (*Elegy*). **Habit / streak field.** Each completed day places one small red dot on a dune-like baseline. Gaps show missed days. The field is the real 60-day record.

16. **Mirror and costume** (*Year of the Cat*). **Tag view.** In a tag view, every row's icon takes the tag's motif, as every dresser object took a cat. The view title reads as the reflection of the tag.

17. **Billiard balls** (*Venus and Mars*). **Two-way comparison / pair picker.** When choosing between two tasks ("which first?"), show two balls, one red and one yellow, each labelled. Pick one and the other rolls away.

18. **Pun from the title** (method, not a single cover). **Section headers.** Give each tab a single literal object from its own name. "Inbox" is a tray. "Someday" is a horizon. Keep it one object, no text beyond the tab label.

19. **Hardie line work** (prism, Deco logo, robots). **Icon set.** Draw icons as single-weight technical line art, as if on a drafting sheet. **(subtle)** This suits a pixel or vector line on beige panels.

20. **Retoucher's mask** (Manning's process). **Edit mode.** In edit mode, everything but the field being edited is masked with a pale wash, as Manning masked all but the glass. Leaving edit mode lifts the mask.

Rules for the blend with retrofuturism:

- Use one tech motif per screen. Hipgnosis covers have one idea each.
- Keep the motif small with lots of empty panel around it.
- Make it real data. Storm's rule was "do it for real". A decorative robot that measures nothing breaks the idea.
- Keep type out of the image. Labels go on the panel, the sticker, or the spine.
