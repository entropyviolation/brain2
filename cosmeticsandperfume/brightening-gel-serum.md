# Brightening gel serum

Humblebee & Me, 23 April 2020. A 50 g batch. Water-based, so a broad-spectrum
preservative is mandatory. The published preservative is 0.25 g (0.5%) Liquid
Germall Plus. This plan uses PE 9010 at 1%, inside the 0.5–1% range and under
the 1.1% ceiling. The extra 0.25 g comes out of the distilled water. The batch
stays 50 g.

Published water was 27 g (54%). This batch uses 26.75 g (53.5%) and 0.50 g
(1%) PE 9010. At 0.5% the weights would match the published line (0.25 g
preservative, 27 g water). One percent matches the shampoo bar and the glitter
mist. PE 9010 dissolves at about 1% in water at 20°C. Pre-disperse it in the
propanediol.

The 10 g hyaluronic line is 0.10 g ELMW powder plus 9.90 g distilled water,
made in the batch. Lotioncrafter does not sell a ready 1% solution. Their jar
labeled LMW is 0.8–1.0 million daltons. Buy ELMW, 80–110 kDa, 1 g, $5.95.
One gram is ten serums. Do not store an unpreserved hyaluronic gel on the shelf.

Weigh the primary phase and whisk. Add the secondary phase and whisk. Add the
water. Cover. Wait at least an hour, overnight is fine, for Aristoflex AVC to
hydrate. Whisk until the gel is thick and creamy. A mini mixer splatters.
Package in the 50 ml airless pump, or a tottle if the pump seal slips. A pea
after cleansing, then an oil or a cream.

When made as written, Marie’s gel lands at pH 5–6, which is where niacinamide
belongs. Test the pH.

## What each piece is doing

Low molecular weight hyaluronic acid, propanediol, and panthenol are the
humectants. Panthenol is also soothing. Niacinamide and N-acetyl glucosamine
are the barrier and brightening pair. Ceramide complex is the barrier mortar
and the TEWL support. CoQ10 is the antioxidant. It colors the gel yellow.
Marie states collagen and UV effects for it. Those stay her wording. They are
cosmetic claims, not medical promises. Allantoin stays at 0.5% because it is
poorly water-soluble and crystallizes above that. Aristoflex AVC thickens the
gel and pseudo-emulsifies the three oil-soluble pieces: Q-MAX, dimethicone 350,
and cyclomethicone.

Q-MAX is 7.5% ubiquinone and 10% tocopheryl acetate in C12-15 alkyl benzoate.
The pure CoQ10 powder is a different material. Leave it off the cart.
Ceramide complex is already preserved with phenoxyethanol and ethylhexylglycerin.
Active matter is 2.5%. Long storage on the Lotioncrafter page is 10–15°C.
The INCI includes sodium lauroyl lactylate. Keep this complex. A casual swap
changes the gel. N-acetyl glucosamine on this page is from crustacean shells.
Lotioncrafter’s suggested range is 1–4%, up to 10%. This formula uses 5%.

Scent is optional. Swap 20–30% of the water for rose hydrosol, about 5.4–8.0 g
of the 26.75 g water line, or cut the water by 0.5–1% for an essential oil.
Four serums at the high end use about 32 g of hydrosol. The 4 oz hydrosol
bottle is the one that still leaves some for the face.

## What stays out

Aloe, sodium lactate, and urea. Aristoflex AVC loses its gel when electrolytes
show up. Heavy dimethicone in place of LC995. Dimethicone 350 is the
non-volatile silicone and stays at 2%. Dimethicone 1.5 is a different oil.
Liquid Germall Plus. A second argan bottle. A second cyclomethicone brand.

Lotioncrafter warns that a high silicone load can lubricate an airless gasket.
This serum is 4% silicone. If the pump fails, use a tottle or a squeeze tube.
The gel is thin enough to pump.

## Safety

Water means PE 9010. Germall Plus is a formaldehyde donor and stays off the cart.
Test pH and keep it at 5–6 for the niacinamide. Allantoin above 0.5% can
crystallize. Aristoflex AVC plus aloe, sodium lactate, or urea can collapse
the gel. N-acetyl glucosamine here is a shellfish-shell ingredient. Neat PE 9010
irritates eyes. Wear glasses when you pour the preservative. The finished serum
is a face product. Keep it out of the eyes.
