# Fabric softener

Laundry, separate from the perfume work. Wash and dryer-ball scents are
in [laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md). The detergent is in
[laundry-detergent.md](laundry-detergent.md). The plan is [plan.md](plan.md).

Not every homemade mix softens clothes.

## You may not need softener

A detergent that rinses clean, plus a dryer, is often enough. Softener
does not clean. Commercial softener coats fibers, which can hurt
absorbency on towels and athletic wear. Glycerin also coats and can
build up, so it stays optional and occasional, not the default.

Hard water leaves a mineral film that makes cloth feel stiff. Line
drying makes that stiffness easier to notice. Softened water, or a
dryer that tumbles the load, often needs nothing extra. Use less
product rather than more. Extra detergent and extra additives leave
their own film.

## Vinegar in the rinse

White vinegar, about 5% acidity, goes in the rinse compartment, not in
the wash with the detergent. It removes leftover detergent and
hard-water mineral film. The vinegar smell leaves after drying.

Dose from the Tracy Ariza note: 1/4 to 1/2 cup per load in the softener
compartment. A larger load or harder water can take the higher end. A
gallon at 1/2 cup is about 32 loads; at 1/4 cup, about 64. If the water
is already softened, use less or skip it. Line-dried clothes feel
stiffer; vinegar helps there. Dryer tumbling already softens.

A few drops of scent in that vinegar are optional. Keep them low.
Distilled white vinegar is a grocery bottle, unpriced, rinse only.

## Diluted mix, optional

Stored as an optional mix, not as the better one. This waters the
vinegar down, so it is weaker than 1/4–1/2 cup of straight vinegar.

| Part | Amount |
|---|---|
| Distilled white vinegar | 2 cups |
| Water | 2 cups |
| Vegetable glycerin | 1 tablespoon, optional |
| Essential oil | 20–30 drops |

Shake it in a glass jar. Use 1/2 cup in the softener dispenser. Glycerin
is the coating risk. Prefer lavender or distilled lemon from the cart.
Eucalyptus is not on the cart and is not a purchase. Do not put this in
the wash cycle with the detergent.

## Wool dryer balls

Wool dryer balls are already on the shopping list, about 6. Use 3 in
the dryer. They soften by separating clothes, not by coating. That
tumbling also lets air through the load, which can shorten the dry and
ease static. They do nothing for a line-dried load.

Scent is a couple of drops of a wash concentrate, not a puddle of neat
oil. Essential oils are flammable. Do not load the balls and immediately
run a hot cycle with a heavy dose. Tide and Gain ball concentrates,
6–8 drops, no lemon on the hot ball, are in
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md). Closet sachets are the no-heat
scent option.

## Tried and skipped

Baking soda is for odor only. Table salt does not earn a place as a
softener.

The Epsom-salt plus baking-soda plus 40-drop blend is a tried-and-skipped
experiment: 2 cups Epsom salt, 1/2 cup baking soda, 40 drops of essential
oil, 3 tablespoons per load in the drum. Salt and Epsom have not shown a
real softening effect. Baking soda is not the softener.

Epsom is already a grocery line for other uses. Do not add baking soda
or vinegar to the priced goods total.

## Athletic wear and towels

Skip coating softeners (glycerin, commercial softener). Vinegar rinse
or dryer balls are the ones that do not leave a film.

Vinegar in the rinse, balls in the dryer, glycerin optional.
