## Kyphi and Ketoret

Kyphi, written kapet in Egyptian, was temple incense. The lists do not
agree. Edfu and later Greek writers describe a ball of resins and spices
bound with raisins, wine, and honey. Labdanum or rockrose appears in some
reconstructions, beside myrrh and frankincense. It is a history of smoke,
not a recipe for this cart.

Ketoret is the daily incense of the Jerusalem temple. Exodus 30:34–36
names four things: stacte, onycha, galbanum, and frankincense, in equal
parts. Rabbinic lists later expand that to eleven, and the plant names
are disputed. Onycha is often read as a sea-snail operculum. Agarwood is
one proposed reading, not a settled spice.

“Lot” in Genesis, sometimes offered as labdanum, is a scholarly guess.
Balm of Gilead is a different tradition. Myrrh, galbanum, agarwood,
onycha, mastic, and cinnamon stay off the shopping list.
