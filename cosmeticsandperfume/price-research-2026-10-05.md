# Price research, 5 October 2026

What was compared, what won, and what was rejected. Figures move. The
shopping list is the cart. This note is why the cart looks like that.

## Method

Naturals were priced from the Eden Botanicals catalog PDF dated
2 October 2026, then checked against live product pages for hinoki,
labdanum clear (2 ml at $7.50), jasmine sambac, lavender high-elevation
($14 for 15 ml), and LC995. The Maillette lavender used here is the
catalog price: 15 ml $11.75, 30 ml $20.50. Thirty milliliters is the
size, because laundry and the shampoo bar will finish 15 ml.

Aroma chemicals were priced from live Perfumer’s Apprentice pages:
Iso E Super, Hedione, ethylene brassylate, Norlimbanol, Cashmeran,
Veramoss, rectified birch tar and its 10% dilution, benzaldehyde,
oakmoss Robertet, guaiacwood, honeysuckle fragrance oil, cherry
fragrance oil, and Base de Parfum.

Pantry ingredients were priced from live Lotioncrafter pages: refined
shea 8 oz $8.75 and 1 lb $13.95, vitamin E T50 1 oz $12.95, PE 9010
$5.95, emulsifying wax NF $8.95, cetearyl alcohol NF $5.75, cetyl
alcohol 8 oz $6.95, beeswax white NF from $7.45, LC995 cyclomethicone
$7.95, Ester AB (C12-15 alkyl benzoate) 8 oz $11.95. Hydrolyzed rice
protein was listed around $4.95–$7.95 for 1–2 oz. SCI and SLSa small
jars were about $5.62 and $5.81 for 2 oz at Wholesale Supplies Plus.
Those jars are short of four shampoo bars. The cart buys Lotioncrafter
SCI at 8 oz, $10.45, and SLSa at 1 lb, $21.95, the smallest jars that
cover four bars. CAPB is 4 oz, $6.25. Polysorbate 20 is 4.5 oz, $5.50.

Plant Therapy 10 ml conifer oils were $11.99–$16.99. Eden’s 15 ml
bottles of fir, cedar, spruce, and cypress were the same money or less,
so the conifers stay at Eden and the order ships once.

## Importing

Fraterworks lists Iso E Super from $3, Hedione from $4, and Cashmeran
from $8.75, with US duties prepaid and free US shipping over $120.
Those opening prices are the smallest size, and several product pages
were marked returning soon. A known 15 ml of Iso E Super at Perfumer’s
Apprentice is $11, with an IFRA sheet, shipped inside the US. These
liquids are flammable. LC995 is already barred from Priority Mail and
from international mail because its flash point is 77°C.

Marketplace oils were rejected on published evidence, not on taste. A
GC-MS study of 28 highly rated Amazon lavender oils found about 89%
failed basic authenticity checks: undisclosed synthetics and diethyl
phthalate in a majority, mineral oil or extenders in a majority, and
species substitution (lavandin sold as lavender) in about half.
Lavandin’s camphor is a different, harsher oil. Cheap fragrance oils
from unvetted craft listings are the same problem: phthalates and
solvents that do not belong in a candle or a shampoo. Perfumer’s
Apprentice and Eden are the vetted houses. Creating Perfume is a real
alternative for benzaldehyde and a cherry accord. It lost on shipping,
because those two bottles already exist at Perfumer’s Apprentice for
$6 each and can ride with the rest of that order.

Liberty Natural is a legitimate house for Virginia cedar and aged
patchouli. Eden’s Virginia cedar is $10.25 for 30 ml and the dark
patchouli is $11.50 for 15 ml, on the same order as the other naturals.
Splitting those two bottles to a fourth supplier costs more than it
saves.

## Choices that look more expensive and are not

- Benzoin absolute, one bottle, then dilute it. A second bottle of
  benzoin already dissolved in propylene glycol is the same smell twice.
  Raw resin is glass-hard and will not pour into a roller.
- Bitter almond only from the Eden listing that states the prussic acid
  has been removed, plus the cherry fragrance oil. Benzaldehyde is the
  molecule and was the cheaper duplicate, so it is not also on the cart.
- Vanillin for the bar and the dryer. Vanilla CO2, 1 ml, for perfume.
  Vanilla absolute near $25 per ml, and a separate coumarin next to
  tonka, were the 90% “savings” that still meant four sweet bottles.
- Turkish rose, 1 ml, not a 10% rose from a fourth shop, and not rose
  otto. Geranium carries soap and laundry.
- Ylang complete, 15 ml, $12.50, not a set of grades I, II, III, extra,
  and the absolute. The catalog’s Ylang II at $10.25 is real and was
  set aside because complete is the full distillation and the price
  gap is about two dollars.
- Amyris, 15 ml, $10. Santalum album at 5 ml is often $80 or more.
  Australian spicatum is about $9.50 for 1 ml when a real sandalwood
  lesson is worth it. Not this order.
- Veramoss instead of raw oakmoss. Robertet’s IFRA oakmoss was $17.75
  for 4 ml and is the only natural oakmoss worth buying later, inside
  the limit on its sheet.
- Norlimbanol, 4 ml, $8.75, covers Ysamber. The 15 ml is $28 and is
  more than a hobby uses.
- Iso E Super, 15 ml, $11. The 50 g is $17 and waits until the first
  bottle is empty, so it does not end up in the wash water.
- PE 9010, $5.95, instead of Liquid Germall Plus. Germall is what the
  rice-bar post was written with. It is a formaldehyde donor, and
  Washington and California rules have made it harder to find. PE 9010
  is phenoxyethanol plus ethylhexylglycerin, effective from about pH 3
  to 12, use 0.5–1%, maximum 1.1%.
- Grapeseed, not Lotioncrafter’s sunflower. That sunflower is high-oleic,
  from about $10.35, and it is a stable oil. The pantry request was a
  linoleic oil: sunflower, safflower, or grapeseed. Grapeseed is the
  one that matches. High-oleic sunflower can join later if something
  needs a more shelf-stable oil.
- Kokum, about $10 for a few ounces, instead of cocoa. Cocoa is the
  brittle butter most recipes name, and poor crops have made it the
  expensive one. Shea cannot stand in for a brittle butter.
- Refined shea, 1 lb, $13.95, not the 8 oz at $8.75. Butters get used
  by the ounce, and refined shea has no smoky smell to fight.
- LC995 cyclomethicone, $7.95, not a generic “cyclomethicone” and not
  LuxGlide on top of it. LC995 is at least 96% D5 and at most 0.099%
  D4. LuxGlide N5 is the silicone-free alternative and a second
  carrier. Alkyl benzoate at 8 oz, $11.95, is the smallest size and
  also a light ester for later lotions.
- Mad Micas Sparkle Plenty sample, $1.49. Synthetic fluorphlogopite.
  TKB Fantasia is a different sparkle and starts higher, and its
  airborne powder carries a California silica and titanium dioxide
  warning that drops once the powder is in liquid. The sample bag is
  the amount a mist needs. The $349 figure on one Mad Micas page was a
  sold-out bulk listing, not the sample.

## Safety lines that removed bottles

Cade, crude or hardware pine tar. Peruvian balsam. Raw oakmoss.
Ambrette seed, because ethylene brassylate does that job and is the
kinder musk for waterways. Blackcurrant bud at $24.25 for 2 ml, and
pineapple isolate as if it were the same smell. Isoamyl laurate as a
third carrier. It remains a possible swap for the alkyl benzoate, and
it cannot replace 69% cyclomethicone. Camellia, rice bran, sweet
almond, mango, cocoa, Olivem 1000, candelilla, carnauba, berry wax.
Neat fossilized amber at about $16 per ml. Neat jasmine. Rose otto.
A 15 ml of real sandalwood.

Bergamot on the cart is FCF. Lemon is the distilled oil. Grapefruit
and sweet orange stay small because expressed citrus oxidizes and
grapefruit is phototoxic in daylight on skin.

Hinoki is Eden’s wood oil, Chamaecyparis obtusa, not the leaf oil.
The page documents plantation sawmill offcuts, not old-growth heartwood.
Tisserand lists no specific hazard beyond dilute and patch-test.
“Clinically proven forest bathing” is a claim about walking among trees
and about the aroma of the forest. The bottle is for smelling and for
diluted skin, hair, and bath use.

## Bottle math

Cute perfume atomizers stay under a few dollars each in a multi-pack
of glass with metal pumps. Labdanum and benzoin need glass droppers
because they glue plastic. The glitter mica is 40–200 microns and gets
a wider mister. The hair mist can use one of the 30 ml glass sprays.
A 20 g pocket scale that reads 0.001 g still wanders in a draft below
about half a gram. The 50 g scale with a cover is the one that can see
0.075 g of hair-mist scent and 0.05 g of diluted birch tar.
