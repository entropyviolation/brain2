# Safety evidence, 5 October 2026

Desk research on the materials named in the formulation plans. It does not change any formula. The standing house review remains [safety-officer.md](../safety-officer.md). This note is the later pass: current standards, set against the percentages already written.

## 1. Method

Read on 5 October 2026: `README.md`, `plan.md`, `shopping-list.md`, `supply-coverage.md`, `cheaper-options.md`, `price-research-2026-10-05.md`, `safety-officer.md`, `perfume-accords-and-safety.md`, `source-paste-review.md`, `global-sourcing-tips.md`, `laundry-detergent.md`, `laundry-scents.md`, `fabric-softener.md`, `perfume-trials.md`, `perfume-experiments.md`, `cedar-palmarosa-solid-perfume.md`, `glitter-mist.md`, `rice-shampoo-bar.md`, `dry-argan-oil-hair-mist.md`, `shimmery-body-oil-spray.md`, `solid-dishwashing-brick.md`, `brightening-gel-serum.md`, and `wishlist-check.md`.

Every named ingredient, oil, aroma chemical, solvent, surfactant, preservative, mica, carrier, and functional additive was listed once. Materials the plans name only to keep them out are in the last block of the table, so they are covered and not researched as if they were in a batch.

Perfume math, used everywhere below:

- A spray is 3 g of a 10 g concentrate in 17 g Base de Parfum. The finished spray is 15% concentrate. Grams in the 10 g table, multiplied by 1.5, are the percent in the spray.
- A roller is 2 g of that concentrate in 8 g jojoba. The finished oil is 20% concentrate. Grams multiplied by 2 are the percent in the roller.
- Solids, the serum, the mists, the shampoo bar, the dish brick, and the Allure botanical rollers are already the finished product. Their table percentages are the use level.
- IFRA Category 4 is fine fragrance, including solid perfume and a non-hydroalcoholic perfume oil. Category 5A is a body oil or body lotion. Category 5B is a face leave-on. Category 7B is a leave-on hair product. Category 9 is rinse-off with body contact. Category 10A is household products with hand contact, such as hand dish and laundry. These are the IFRA category meanings used by the 51st Amendment guidance; a supplier certificate that reprints them is cited under bitter orange.

Limits are from the IFRA Standards, 51st Amendment, notified 30 June 2023, unless a later regulator page is named. The 52nd Amendment had closed consultation and was not yet the notified standard on the IFRA pages opened for this note. One page dated 15 June 2026 expected notification toward the end of November 2026. The end-of-consultation letter expected the formal notification in January 2027. Nothing below uses a 52nd Amendment number.

This is not a toxicology review, a patch test, or a certificate of analysis. Where a lot’s composition is unpublished, the comparison uses a published range or an IFRA “maximum found” figure, and it says so. Hazard is what the substance can do. Risk here means that hazard at the dose these plans actually write.

## 3. Per material

### Benzaldehyde and bitter almond

The IFRA standard is the strongest new number. Category 4, which includes solid perfume and a perfume roller, is 0.25% benzaldehyde in the finished product. Category 5A, a body oil, is 0.064%. The standard lists bitter almond oil, synthetic, among the synonyms, and the driving endpoints are dermal sensitization and systemic toxicity.

The amber cherry tin is 0.50% bitter almond before anyone counts the cherry fragrance oil. The Lost Cherry spray is 0.30% and its roller is 0.40% when the concentrate uses 0.20 g. Those are over 0.25% if the oil is counted as benzaldehyde. The Allure trial at 0.08 g stays under Category 4. The file’s 0.15 g “cap” for the tin would be 1.5%, which is not a safe ceiling under this standard.

FFPA means the prussic acid has been taken out. It does not mean the oil has stopped being benzaldehyde, and it does not make the oil a food. A COA that shows the oil is far below 100% benzaldehyde would change the arithmetic. Until then, the conservative reading is the one in the table.

### Cherry, honeysuckle, coconut, green tea, and fossilized amber

These are fragrance oils or a 5% specialty oil. Their restricted constituents are not public. Cherry is the one that sits on top of a bitter-almond line that is already over the benzaldehyde limit. The others are gaps, not demonstrated exceedances. An IFRA certificate for each bottle is the missing document. The plan’s own 3% note on Coconut II in the roller was not verified against a standard, because the mixture is unpublished.

### Ylang ylang

The house ceiling of about 0.8% on skin is slightly looser than IFRA Category 4, which is 0.73%. The Pure Poison roller, at 0.40 g in the 10 g concentrate, is 0.80% ylang in the finished oil. The spray of that same concentrate is 0.60% and meets Category 4. The honeysuckle wearable roller at 0.35 g is 0.70% and meets it.

Body oil is tighter: Category 5A is 0.18%. The shimmer’s 0.7% scent line cannot be ylang. The oils that file already names (lavender, hinoki, bergamot FCF, Virginia cedar) are the ones that fit a body oil. A leave-on hair mist is Category 7B, where ylang is allowed at 1.5%, so a thread inside the 0.3% scent line is fine. The shampoo bar and the Gain scoop are rinse-off or household dilutions and sit under Category 9 and 10A.

### Clove and eugenol

The 2023 eugenol standard allows 2.5% eugenol in Category 4 and 0.64% in Category 5A. The fireplace doses are 0.12–0.20% clove oil in the finished perfume. The house rule of 0.5% clove oil on skin is stricter than Category 4 and still sensible for a body oil, because clove bud oil is mostly eugenol. Nothing written for wear exceeds either number. Paste lines that scale clove toward 0.6 g in a 10 g concentrate are already marked not to wear. That mark matches the math.

### Tonka and coumarin

Coumarin in Category 4 is 1.5%. The IFRA annex maximum found in tonka absolute is 46.7% coumarin. The heaviest roller variant, 0.90 g tonka, is about 0.84% coumarin at that maximum. That is under Category 4. It would be over Category 5A, so this tonka stays in perfume, not in the shimmer oil or the serum. The amber cherry solid at 1% tonka is about 0.47% coumarin. Tonka is not a flavoring ingredient. Dihydrocoumarin’s Category 4 limit is 0.21%; at the annex maximum of 0.05% in the absolute, the contribution is far below that.

### Cashmeran, Iso E Super, and Norlimbanol

Cashmeran at 1.1–1.4% of a perfume is under the Category 4 limit of 3.8% and over the body-oil limit of 0.31%. The Goddess-lean solid is a solid perfume, so Category 4 applies. It should not be recast as a body balm. Laundry is Category 10A at 0.13%, which is why cashmeran stays out of the wash concentrates. That household limit is new relative to the aquatic note the plans already make for Iso E Super.

Iso E Super in the clean-woods roller is about 5%, under the 20% Category 4 limit. The wash exclusion is an environmental choice. It is not required by the dermal standard at the laundry scoop, which would be far under the 2.4% Category 10A figure.

Norlimbanol in the clean-woods accord is a trace of the neat material and is far under the 1.3% Category 4 limit for 1-(2,2,6-trimethylcyclohexyl)-3-hexanol. The bottle’s CAS should be checked against that standard.

### Jasmine

Sambac absolute is allowed at 3.8% in Category 4. The heaviest cart roller is about 0.72% absolute. Grandiflorum is allowed at 0.60%. The decision to buy sambac 10%, and not grandiflorum, is a real limit difference, not only a smell difference. The 10% dilution is in fractionated coconut. That is a solubility problem in alcohol, which the plans already describe. It does not raise the absolute percentage.

### Citrus, phototoxicity, and oxidized limonene

Expressed bergamot is capped at 0.40% on leave-on skin. Cold-pressed lemon is capped at 2.0%. Bitter orange peel is capped at 1.25%. Grapefruit is capped at 4.0%. The Illicit extra variant, at 1.00% bitter orange in the roller and 0.75% in the spray, is under 1.25%. The night grapefruit lines, at 1.6% and 2.0% of a roller, are under 4.0%. Night-only wear is stricter than the grapefruit standard. It is the right habit if the bergapten content is unknown, and it is how the combination rule stays simple: phototoxic oils in one product share a single budget.

Bergamot FCF and distilled lemon are the grades that are supposed to avoid those caps. FCF still wants a bergapten number on the COA. Sweet orange is not in that phototoxic set. Its problem is limonene hydroperoxides. IFRA’s limonene and linalool standards are specifications: peroxide value under 20 mmol/L, with an antioxidant such as 0.1% tocopherol doing the work in the standard’s own text. The Allure botanical roller that is 4% sweet orange should be made from fresh oil and kept out of a hot windowsill. “Off sunlit skin” is extra caution for sweet orange. The evidence-based concern is oxidized limonene on skin, including skin that is not in the sun.

### Palmarosa and geraniol

The 2023 geraniol limit is 4.7% in Category 4. The cedar tin is about 1.1% palmarosa. That is under the geraniol cap even under the fiction that the oil is pure geraniol. No revision.

### Rose, nutmeg, methyl eugenol, and safrole

Methyl eugenol in a fine fragrance is 0.01% under EU Annex III, entry 102, and 0.011% under IFRA Category 4. Published rose-absolute levels around 0.4–0.8% methyl eugenol keep the heaviest roller (0.80% rose in the oil) under those caps. Distilled rose oil can be several percent methyl eugenol. The cart buys a by-absolute. A COA should say which, and should list methyl eugenol.

Nutmeg at 0.08 g stays under the 0.01% safrole note and under the fine-fragrance methyl eugenol cap, using the EFSA batch ranges (safrole 1.69–2.30%, methyl eugenol 0.18–0.33%). Myristicin in those batches was 8.3–10.7% of the oil, which is about 0.016% of the roller. That is not an IFRA dermal limit. It is a reason not to enlarge the nutmeg line or to eat the oil.

### Birch tar

Crude birch-wood pyrolysate is prohibited. Rectified oil may be used only when benzo[a]pyrene and 1,2-benzanthracene, combined, are at or under 1 ppb in the finished product. The 0.05 g of a 10% dilution is a small mass. It does not answer the specification. Fireplace concentrates should wait on a PAH result for that bottle. Cade stays unbought for the same PAH reason. Cats and birds should not lick surfaces that carry clove or this tar. The metabolic background is poor glucuronidation of phenols in cats, shown for acetaminophen by Court and Greenblatt in 1997, and then applied by veterinarians to phenolic oils. It is not a study of this 0.05 g dose.

### Veramoss and oakmoss

Oakmoss extracts are limited to 0.10% in Category 4 and are specified for atranol and chloroatranol. Veramoss is the substitute in the men’s accord, at a thread. A certificate for that aromatic chemical is still worth having. This pass did not find a separate percentage standard under the name Veramoss.

### Preservative

Phenoxyethanol at 1.0% is the Annex V maximum, and SCCS/1575/16 accepted that maximum, including for children, on cosmetic exposure alone. PE 9010 is a blend, so a 1% dose of the blend is less than 1% phenoxyethanol. Confirm the split before anyone treats 1.1% of the blend as automatically legal. Neat PE 9010 in the eye is an irritant. The finished serum, mist, and bar are the diluted product. Germall Plus stays out because it is a formaldehyde donor.

### Silicones

Regulation (EU) 2024/1328 extends the D4/D5/D6 restriction. Leave-on cosmetics have a derogation until 6 June 2027. Today’s date is before that, and these batches are not being placed on the EU market. The hair mist is still 69.2% D5, and the serum is 2%. The environmental reason to keep them out of the sink is unchanged. ECHA states that D4 is prohibited in EU cosmetics because of reproductive toxicity. The LC995 spec, D4 at or under 0.099% of the silicone, is the control on that impurity. The flash point, 77°C, is why the hair mist is not the pan that melts the shimmer wax.

### Borax

Disodium tetraborate is Repr. 1B, H360FD, under CLP. The 0.3% figure is the generic limit for when a mixture must carry that classification. It is not a laundry dose that a risk assessment in this pass found acceptable. Keeping borax off the everyday wash matches the classification. The one-bag test is still that powder. The practical exposure while measuring it is dust.

### Serum actives

Niacinamide at 4% sits inside the clinical range CIR cited in 2005 (use tests up to 5% without irritation in the studies they summarized). N-acetyl glucosamine at 5% is the highest leave-on level CIR reported in 2022, and the conclusion is “safe as used when formulated to be non-irritating.” The crustacean origin is real. A person with shellfish allergy should know it is there. This pass did not find a rate for topical reactions. Allantoin above 0.5% is a crystallization problem. The open 0.5–1% essential-oil slot in a face gel is Category 5B, where ylang is 0.18% and citral is 0.15%. That slot should stay lavender-scale, or empty, until a named oil is checked. Rose hydrosol in place of some of the water is a much lower allergen load than an essential oil.

### Surfactants and the dish brick

SCI at 40% is under the 53% rinse-off use CIR described in 2013. The plan’s 49.87% figure was not confirmed as a regulatory cap. CAPB at 11% of the liquid needs an activity number and a DMAPA/amidoamine number; the sensitization risk lives in those impurities. SLSa, SCS, carrageenan, and washing soda are dust hazards while dry and, in the brick, an alkaline cleaner. The brick is 56.4% sodium coco sulfate and 27% sodium carbonate. It is not a skin product. Eyes, aluminum, and the dishwasher are the accidents. A bar by the sink can be eaten by a child or a pet. That is storage, not a percentage revision.

### Mica and glitter

CIR concluded in 2012 that synthetic fluorphlogopite is safe as used, and it discussed spray products at about 0.05–2%. The glitter mist is 0.5% and is a pump. Sparkle Plenty’s 40–200 µm particles are larger than the respirable dust the panel was distinguishing from cosmetic sprays. Wet the powder. Do not spray it into eyes, and do not blow the dry jar toward a bird or a cat. HiLite Copper is mined mica. 21 CFR 73.1496 requires lead ≤ 20 ppm, arsenic ≤ 3 ppm, and mercury ≤ 1 ppm. The COA is the evidence. Plastic glitter stays out.

### Carriers, waxes, and vitamin E

Argan at 20%, grapeseed, jojoba, beeswax, shea, glycerin, and vitamin E at 0.5% do not cross a use limit found in this pass. Argan’s safety problem in commerce is a cut or oxidized oil. Smell and a COA are the checks. Kokum, cetearyl alcohol, cetyl alcohol, stearic acid, and emulsifying wax are purchased and not yet in a formula. Crystal Clear Wax contributes about 0.15% polyurethane-79. That is a data gap at a low level, not a cited exceedance.

### Laundry, dryer, vinegar, and pets

Basketter and colleagues (Contact Dermatitis, 2006) judged the risk of inducing fragrance allergy from fragrance left on washed fabric to be extremely low. Steinemann (Air Quality, Atmosphere & Health, 2016) documented terpene emissions from fragranced products and self-reported symptoms, including symptoms people attributed to dryer vents. Those are not the same kind of evidence. The plans’ wash dose is 0.5–1% concentrate in a 30 g scoop, then a full rinse. The dryer ball is the more concentrated inhalation: 6–8 drops, heated. Lemon is already off the hot ball. Clove, birch tar, and Iso E Super are already out of the wash. Ylang stays in the Gain scoop and off the ball, which also keeps the heated dose away from a sensitizer that is capped at 0.73% on skin.

Vinegar at about 5% in the rinse is an acid for film and odor. It is an eye irritant in the bottle. It is not a hormone active, and it does not belong in the same cycle as washing soda. Sodium percarbonate is the oxygen bleach for rags. It stays off skin and out of the vinegar cup. Glycerin as a softener coats cloth; that is a textile problem, not a toxicology ceiling. The optional 20–30 drop softener mix is a larger essential-oil dose than the dryer-ball rule. If that jar is mixed, lavender or distilled lemon matches the rest of the plan. Eucalyptus is not on the cart.

### Pregnancy, infants, and ingestion

Borax is the ingredient with a harmonised reproductive-toxicity classification. Benzaldehyde and coumarin are the perfume materials with both sensitization limits and a clear “not food” rule. Cardamom in the solid is about 1%. The infant literature on 1,8-cineole is about swallowed or nasal eucalyptus oil, in milliliter amounts (Tibballs, 1995). The matching precaution is: do not put this tin, peppermint, or any cineole-rich oil on a baby’s face or in the nose, and do not leave the bottles where a child can drink them. It is not a reason to rewrite the adult 1% cardamom line on the evidence opened here. A pregnancy prohibition for these perfume dilutions was not supported by a study in this pass.

## 4. Formula flags

Revise these before they are worn. The rest of the plans are at or under the limits this pass could cite.

1. **Amber cherry solid** (`cedar-palmarosa-solid-perfume.md`). Bitter almond is 0.05 g in 10.00 g, **0.50%**, against an IFRA Category 4 benzaldehyde limit of **0.25%**. The 0.15 g cap written in that file would be 1.5%. Cherry fragrance oil at 0.90 g (9%) may add more benzaldehyde. Bring the bitter-almond line down so that almond plus whatever the cherry oil’s certificate declares stays at or under 0.25% of the tin. A swipe is still the whole formula’s concentration under IFRA; the limit is a percentage of the product, not of the swipe.

2. **Lost Cherry trial** (`perfume-trials.md`). Bitter almond 0.20 g. Spray **0.30%**, roller **0.40%**, both over 0.25%, and the 3.50 g cherry fragrance oil is extra uncounted benzaldehyde. The spray needs the almond at or under about 0.16 g in the 10 g concentrate to land on 0.25%, and lower if the fragrance oil contributes. The roller needs a lower concentrate dose again, because the roller is 20% concentrate rather than 15%.

3. **Hypnotic Poison experiment** (`perfume-experiments.md`) at 0.20 g bitter almond. Same 0.30% / 0.40% problem.

4. **Hypnotic Poison trial** (`perfume-trials.md`) and the **Cinéma experiment** at 0.15 g. The spray is 0.225%, under 0.25%. The roller is **0.30%**, over. Wear the spray, or cut the roller’s concentrate, until a COA shows the oil is dilute enough that 0.30% of the oil is still ≤ 0.25% benzaldehyde.

5. **Allure Homme Sport trial** at 0.08 g bitter almond. Spray 0.12% and roller 0.16% meet Category 4. Leave this one. Do not move it into a body oil: Category 5A is 0.064%.

6. **Pure Poison roller** (`perfume-trials.md`). Ylang complete 0.40 g becomes **0.80%** of the roller. IFRA Category 4 is **0.73%**. The spray of the same concentrate is 0.60% and meets the limit. Drop the roller to the honeysuckle trial’s 0.35 g (0.70%) or lower, or wear the spray.

7. **Fireplace trial, Fireplace experiment, and the burnt accord.** Birch tar is 0.05 g of a 10% dilution. Do not treat “rectified” as the finished-product PAH spec. Use it when the supplier shows benzo[a]pyrene plus 1,2-benzanthracene at or under 1 ppb in the finished spray and the finished roller. Crude cade and crude tar stay out.

8. **Brightening gel, the unnamed 0.5–1% essential-oil option.** A face gel is Category 5B. Ylang’s limit there is 0.18%, and citral’s is 0.15%. Do not put ylang, clove, lemongrass, or expressed citrus in that slot. Lavender at a thread, or the rose-hydrosol option, matches the limits. The 4% niacinamide and 5% N-acetyl glucosamine do not exceed the CIR figures cited above.

9. **Shimmer spray, if the 0.7% scent line is swapped.** Category 5A allows ylang only to 0.18% and citral only to 0.15%. The written choices (lavender, hinoki, bergamot FCF, Virginia cedar) are the ones that fit. Bergamot in that bottle has to stay FCF.

10. **Paste tables in `perfume-experiments.md` that the file already tells you not to wear.** Scaled bitter almond, scaled clove, cade, and Peru balsam would break the benzaldehyde limit, the eugenol limit, or an IFRA prohibition. The cart gram tables are the ones to make, subject to the flags above.

Not flags, because the comparison came out under the cited limit: palmarosa at about 1.1% versus geraniol 4.7%; bitter orange at 1.00% of a roller versus 1.25%; grapefruit at 1.6–2.0% versus 4.0%; clove at the fireplace doses versus eugenol 2.5% / 0.64%; sambac absolute at 0.72% versus 3.8%; tonka-as-coumarin under 1.5% in Category 4; Cashmeran at 1.1–1.4% versus 3.8% in Category 4; SCI at 40% versus reported rinse-off use of 53%; phenoxyethanol in a 1% blend versus a 1.0% cap on phenoxyethanol itself; glitter mica at 0.5% versus the CIR spray discussion.

## 5. Gaps a person still has to close

- Patch-test on the inner forearm, and wait, before a full wear of jasmine, ylang, tonka, benzoin, pink pepper, clove, and birch tar. A desk review cannot see a person who is already sensitized. Elicitation can happen below the induction limit IFRA used.
- COA or IFRA certificate, with the constituent named: bitter almond (benzaldehyde assay and prussic-acid statement), cherry fragrance oil (benzaldehyde and any other restricted materials), honeysuckle, Coconut II, green tea, fossilized amber 5%, bergamot FCF (bergapten), ylang, Turkish rose by-absolute (methyl eugenol), nutmeg (safrole and methyl eugenol), rectified birch tar (benzo[a]pyrene and 1,2-benzanthracene), Veramoss, Norlimbanol (CAS), PE 9010 (phenoxyethanol percentage), CAPB (activity, DMAPA, amidoamine), polysorbate 20 and SCI (1,4-dioxane), HiLite Copper (lead, arsenic, mercury against 21 CFR 73.1496), LC995 (D4, D5, D6), argan (identity, not a cut oil).
- Peroxide value on citrus, lavender, and the needle oils if they are old. The IFRA figure is under 20 mmol/L for limonene and for linalool.
- The 52nd Amendment, once IFRA notifies it. Phototoxicity and furocoumarin rules are among the pieces under revision. Re-check bitter orange, grapefruit, and bergamot FCF when that notification exists.
- This pass did not open human patch-test tables for every oil. LLNA was not used as a primary limit. Where the evidence is a classification, a CIR “safe as used,” or an absence of a standard, the table says so.
