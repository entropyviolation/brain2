# Efficacy evidence, 5 October 2026

What each material is being asked to do in these plans, and whether evidence supports that job near the formula’s dose. This note does not change any formula. Safety limits stay in [safety-officer.md](../safety-officer.md).

## 1. Method note

Read on 5 October 2026: the plan, the shopping and coverage notes, and every product file named in the request (laundry detergent and scents, fabric softener, perfume trials and experiments, accords, the solid perfumes, glitter mist, rice shampoo bar, dry argan hair mist, shimmery body oil, dishwashing brick, brightening serum, wishlist check).

Each ingredient was graded for the job that file gives it, at a dose in that file’s ballpark:

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Supported | A clinical trial, a regulatory monograph, or a primary technical source matches this job near this dose. |
| Plausible | The mechanism or a neighboring dose fits, or perfumery practice is consistent, and this exact batch has not been tested. |
| Weak | The claim runs ahead of the data, the tests disagree, or the studied dose is far from the formula. |
| Unsupported | The evidence does not support that job. |

Cosmetic grades prefer randomized trials, the FDA skin-protectant monograph, CIR conclusions used only where they speak to function, and SCCS or supplier preservative data. Fragrance grades are not clinical trials. They rest on volatility, odor-reference compilations (The Good Scents Company, Arctander as quoted by RIFM), and manufacturer performance sheets (IFF, Firmenich). A blotter time is not a promise of how a washed shirt will smell. Cleaning grades rest on detergent chemistry (American Cleaning Institute, textile detergency research) plus the dose arithmetic in the plans.

Numbers below are either taken from those sources or calculated from the gram tables already in the plans. No performance percentage was invented. Preservation of a specific jar is not proven until that jar passes a challenge test.

## 3. Per product

### Brightening gel serum

What earns its place: niacinamide at 4%. That is the dose in a split-face melasma trial against 4% hydroquinone, and 5% improved spots, yellowing, and wrinkles against vehicle in a 12-week split-face study. Two percent has cut water loss in atopic dry skin and in a rosacea-moisturizer forearm test. The barrier story is also mechanistic: nicotinamide raises ceramide and other stratum-corneum lipids in keratinocytes. Panthenol at 2% sits between the 1% and 5% levels that reduced water loss. Dimethicone at 2% is inside the 1–30% skin-protectant monograph. Allantoin at 0.5% is the monograph floor. PE 9010 at 1% is the right preservative class for a water gel. Aristoflex earns its place as the gel, provided electrolytes stay out.

Soften these claims: “Niacinamide and N-acetyl glucosamine are the barrier and brightening pair.” The pair that was tested is 4% niacinamide plus 2% N-acetyl glucosamine. This jar is 4% plus 5%. Pigment improvement at 2% glucosamine is real, including a 202-person vehicle-controlled trial. A barrier claim for the glucosamine is not. CoQ10 should be described as a skin antioxidant at about 0.15% ubiquinone. Hoppe’s wrinkle-depth result used 0.3% for six months in 20 people. Knott showed higher skin ubiquinone from much lower micromolar formulas. Collagen and UV wording from the source recipe is ahead of this dose, and the serum is not a sunscreen. The ceramide line is a reasonable lipid supplement around a tenth of a percent if “2.5% active matter” means ceramide, and that arithmetic should be checked on the lot’s specification. A 2023 meta-analysis found ceramide moisturizers improved eczema scores more than other moisturizers, and did not find a clear water-loss advantage. Hyaluronic acid at 0.2% is a fair humectant. The trial that separated molecular weights used 0.1% of 7 kDa versus 1,800 kDa. This powder is 80–110 kDa. Propanediol at 2% is a solvent with a modest humectant effect. Rose hydrosol is optional scent.

### Dry argan oil hair mist

What earns its place: cyclopentasiloxane as the volatile carrier, the alkyl benzoate as the light ester that remains, argan as the non-volatile oil, and vitamin E as the antioxidant. After the silicone evaporates, most of what is left is argan. That architecture is sound. Argan pretreatment has reduced protein loss from oxidized hair, and an argan conditioner has helped bleached hair keep tensile strength. Those were not tests of a 20% dry mist.

Soften: any reading of this bottle as a hair treatment, growth oil, or silicone “seal.” The silicone is the part that leaves. Jojoba would be a more oxidation-stable substitute and a different smell. Grapeseed is the linoleic carrier elsewhere; it is not a better hair active than argan.

### Rice shampoo bar

What earns its place: SCI as the mild, creamy surfactant, SLSa as the faster foam, and betaine as the foam booster. That trio is a real syndet wash. Grapeseed at 4% is a small emollient. Vitamin E protects that oil a little. PE 9010 is required because the betaine and the protein bring water. The scent blend (lavender, cedar, a little ylang or geranium, a rinse-off dose of sweet orange) is a reasonable wash-off perfume. Hedione, jasmine, and rose would be wasted here, as the file already says.

Soften: rice as a hair benefit. The starch is a filler. The 2019 rice-peptide shampoo (2% in a liquid) did not significantly increase shine, and combing force increased. Four percent of a liquid protein in a bar that is rinsed off is a weak conditioning dose. Call the bar a mild syndet. Do not call it a rice treatment.

### Glitter mist

What earns its place: water, glycerin at 5% as a humectant, polysorbate to wet 0.5% synthetic fluorphlogopite, and PE 9010. The sparkle is optical. Coarse particles need the wide nozzle. Shake-before-use is part of the function.

Soften: nothing biological. Optional hinoki or lavender is scent only. If the scent is pushed toward 1% while polysorbate stays at 1%, expect some oil to float. That is a solubilizer shortfall, not a skin benefit.

### Shimmery body oil spray

What earns its place: grapeseed and jojoba as the emollient body, vitamin E because grapeseed is linoleic, fine mica at 1% for shimmer, and Crystal Clear Wax if the goal is a shimmer that stays up. The 2013 shea version earns emollience and loses suspension.

Soften: any treatment claim for grapeseed or shea. Fragrance at 0.7% has to be a material that is comfortable at 0.7% on skin. The file’s lavender, hinoki, FCF bergamot, and Virginia cedar fit a scent job. They are not actives.

### Solid dishwashing brick

What earns its place: sodium coco sulfate as the cleaner, sodium carbonate as alkali and hardness control, and the glucoside as a wetting helper. That combination should cut ordinary kitchen grease better than the shampoo bar. Glycerin at 3.7% is a press plasticizer. PE 9010 at 0.5% is the bottom of the preservative range and is justified because the brick gets wet. High anionic load, low water, and high pH make self-preservation plausible. They do not make the preservative optional.

Soften: iota carrageenan as the thing that “sets” the brick. The cation and the water are a poor match for an iota gel. Hardness will come mostly from a dry, pressed surfactant powder. This is a dish cleaner, not a skin wash, and not a laundry detergent.

### Laundry detergent

What earns its place: a fragrance-free enzyme powder as the everyday wash. Sodium carbonate as a booster beside that powder. Sodium percarbonate as a separate oxygen bleach for whites and oxidizable stains when the water is warm. The cart pinch (15 g carbonate and 1 g SLSa or SCI) is a fair light-soil experiment and a poor copy of a built detergent.

Soften, in line with what the file already argues: grated soap, borax, and dish soap as the household detergent. Lime soap is real chemistry, not a blog scare. Vinegar does not belong in the same cycle as washing soda. Optical brighteners are dyes. Enzymes are the cold-water stain tools the homemade bags leave out.

### Laundry scents

What earns its place: ethylene brassylate as the part most likely to remain on cloth, Hedione as the radiant floral, Virginia cedar as a woody thread, citrus and sweet orange as in-wash tops that will mostly be gone after the rinse, geranium as the wash rose, vanillin as a pinch of sweetness on the Gain ball. The split between a citrus-heavy wash dose and a musk-heavy dryer-ball dose matches volatility. Ylang and vanillin stay off a hot ball, or stay tiny, because heat makes them loud. That judgment is sound.

Soften: “Tide-shaped” and “Gain-shaped” as matches to the brands. The classic aldehydic laundry bite is not on the cart. There is no apple material. These concentrates can smell like clean soap or fruity floral laundry. They are not those products. A 0.15–0.30 g charge in a wash is a light fragrance dose. Expect a soft dry-cloth smell, led by the musk, not a room-filling citrus.

Iso E Super would cling to fabric and is correctly kept out of the washer in these plans. Galaxolide is not required for this job; ethylene brassylate is the musk that was chosen.

### Fabric softener

What earns its place: vinegar in the rinse as a possible residue and odor helper, used apart from the alkaline wash. Dryer balls as non-coating tumblers. The decision to skip glycerin, Epsom, baking soda, and salt as softeners is the evidence-backed part of this file.

Soften: balls as a reliable softener or static cure, and vinegar as a disinfectant or a stain remover. Commercial cationic softener really does soften, and it really does coat towels. These plans trade that softening for absorbency. That is a coherent choice. It is not the same as proving that vinegar or wool makes cloth soft.

### Perfume trials, experiments, accords, and the solid tins

What earns its place: the low-volatility materials that make a natural-heavy blend last at all. Ethylene brassylate, vanillin, benzoin, labdanum, tonka, vetiver, guaiac, cedar, amyris-as-wood, and Iso E Super in the alcohol sprays. Hedione at a high share of the concentrate is the correct radiant floral, especially where the sambac absolute dose is tiny. Petitgrain is the right daylight orange-blossom substitute. FCF bergamot and distilled lemon are the right citrus for skin that sees sun. The botanical roller’s own warning is accurate: a 10% natural oil sits close to the skin, needs reapplying, and will not last eight hours. The alcohol Allure trial, with Iso E Super, musk, tonka, and cedar, is the longer-lived version of that idea.

Beeswax and grapeseed earn their place as the tin. They are not the perfume. A swipe is a low scent throw next to a 15% alcohol spray. That is structure, not a failure of the cedar or the palmarosa.

Soften:

- Amyris wherever the note list says sandalwood. It is a useful wood. It is not santalol. The Australian spicatum trace is the only true sandalwood in the tables, and it is a trace.
- Benzoin wherever the note list says honey or caramel, unless honey lactone or ethyl maltol is actually in that jar.
- Fragrance oils wherever the note list says tuberose, rangoon creeper, black cherry, jasmine tea, or coconut. They are stand-ins.
- Bitter almond as anything but a volatile almond top. It does not anchor Hypnotic Poison, and it is not a sleep material.
- Jasmine sambac in the light Illicit table. The heavy Bloom-style doses earn the flower. The 0.30 g of a 10% dilution does not.
- Atlas cedar at 0.10 g, fossilized amber at an effective 0.04 g, and Norlimbanol at a neat 0.015 g in 10 g. They are colors. The accords still stand if those whispers are quiet.
- Any “eight hour sillage” reading of the chart-led grams. The Fragrantica bars were a weighing guide. They are not performance data for these materials.

Hinoki, palmarosa, cardamom, clove, birch tar, fir, and vetiver earn their places as the notes those formulas say they are. They do not earn therapeutic claims.

## 4. Redundancies

A buyer can drop some of these without losing the effect the plans actually have evidence for.

| Overlap | What to keep for the stated job | What can go quiet |
|---|---|---|
| Brightening | Niacinamide at 4% | N-acetyl glucosamine above 2% is unproven extra. At a tight budget the glucosamine is the optional half of a pair that was tested at 2%. |
| Barrier and water | Niacinamide, panthenol, dimethicone | Ceramide complex is the expensive plausible. Hyaluronic acid and propanediol humect; they do not replace the barrier trio. |
| Antioxidant in the serum versus in the oils | CoQ10 for skin; vitamin E for the oils | They are not substitutes. Tocopheryl acetate inside Q-MAX does not protect grapeseed. |
| Hair “treatment” | Argan in the dry mist, if one oil is the point | Rice starch is filler. Rice protein is not pulling its weight for shine. Jojoba can replace argan if the goal is a stable carrier rather than the argan studies. |
| Bar foam | SCI if the goal is mild and creamy; SLSa if the goal is fast foam | Dropping either changes the lather. Dropping both ends the cleanser. Betaine is the booster, not a second cleanser. |
| Dish versus hair surfactant | SCS at the sink, SCI on hair | They are not interchangeable. |
| Laundry cleaning | Enzyme powder, carbonate as booster, percarbonate when hot | The 1 g SLSa pinch, grated soap, borax, dish soap, baking soda. |
| Softening without a film | Rinse vinegar for residue; balls so nothing coats the towel | Glycerin, Epsom, salt, baking soda. Balls are optional if softness was the hope. |
| Fabric scent that lasts | Ethylene brassylate, then a little Hedione and cedar | Citrus on the dryer ball. Ylang on the ball. Iso E Super is substantive and stays out of the washer by choice. |
| Sandalwood versus wood | Amyris or cedar or Iso E Super for “a wood that lasts” | Do not expect amyris to do spicatum’s job. Atlas at 0.10 g is skippable. |
| Honey and caramel | Honey lactone and ethyl maltol in the one variant that uses them | Benzoin still earns a fixative place. It does not replace those two. |
| Vanilla | Vanillin for tenacity, laundry, and every trial the 1 ml cannot fund | Vanilla CO2 for a single richer perfume. Owning both is a smell choice, not a double treatment. |
| Rose | Geranium in wash and soap; Turkish rose in a perfume that uses at least about 0.14 g in 10 g | Rose hydrosol does not do either job. |
| Jasmine | Hedione for radiance and for laundry; sambac when the absolute dose is in the Bloom range | Trace sambac in the light Illicit table. |
| Coconut | Coconut II if the smell matters | Fractionated coconut oil, which is there to dissolve absolutes. |
| Glitter | Sparkle Plenty in the wide water mist; HiLite Copper in the oil mister | One cannot do the other’s nozzle job. |
| Shimmer suspension | Crystal Clear Wax | The shea fallback is the spare, and the mica will settle. |
| Unused wardrobe | Nothing, until a formula weighs them | Patchouli, frankincense, Scotch pine, kokum, and the loose fatty alcohols and emulsifying wax. |

## 5. Gaps

- None of these finished jars has a preservative challenge test. PE 9010 at 0.5–1% is the right range, and some products preserved that way still grow mold.
- No wash-and-dry panel has smelled these concentrates. The laundry design follows volatility. It is not a substantivity measurement.
- No combing, shine, or protein-loss measurement exists for the argan mist or the shampoo bar.
- The ceramide lot’s “2.5% active matter” needs a specification check before anyone treats 0.125% as the ceramide dose.
- Q-MAX wrinkle, collagen, and UV claims are not matched to 0.15% ubiquinone.
- N-acetyl glucosamine has human pigment data at 2%, not at 5%, and little barrier data.
- Vinegar’s bacterial data sit at roughly 0.3–0.75% acetic acid in the wash. The rinse dose is much lower, and it is a different cycle.
- Dryer-ball tests disagree with each other and are mostly home or review-lab cycles, not textile-lab softness standards.
- Iota carrageenan’s gel behavior in this low-water sodium brick has not been measured.
- Fragrance oils have no public composition, so tenacity and “is it really honeysuckle” cannot be graded past the label.
- The Hedione bottle is written as Hedione, not Hedione HC. High-cis tenacity numbers do not automatically apply.
- Patchouli, frankincense, and Scotch pine are purchased smells without a weighed job.
