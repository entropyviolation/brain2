# Laundry detergent

Laundry, separate from the perfume work. Wash scents are in
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md). Vinegar and dryer balls are in
[fabric-softener.md](fabric-softener.md). The plan is [plan.md](plan.md).
The standing review is [safety-officer.md](safety-officer.md).

The dish brick is a dish cleaner. It is not laundry detergent.

Soap and detergent are different. A grated bar is traditional soap:
fats and lye. It is a surfactant, so it is not just baking soda. With
washing soda and borax, that bag has a surfactant, a chelator (washing
soda), and a pH buffer (borax). It still lacks enzymes, anti-redeposition
polymers, and an oxidizer. The laundry thread’s words for those polymers
are suspension agents, and polymers that keep dirt from settling back
on the cloth. A detergent is built to rinse, and to foam little.
Commercial powder is built for cold water and a low-water HE machine.
Soap wants hot water and real agitation. That is why a washboard and a
bar used to be enough. Hot water also wears cloth and costs energy.
(Reddit laundry thread; Kathryn Kellogg, 27 July 2020; Green Llama.)
The default below is that kind of powder. Borax, grated laundry soap, and dish soap are optional tests.
They are not the everyday mix.

## Powder yes, soap powder no

Grated soap powder plus washing soda will not be the plan. Kathryn
Kellogg’s result, 27 July 2020, is the reason.

A powder can still be the wash if the surfactant is a detergent
surfactant, not a soap. On this cart that is a small amount of SLSa or
SCI, already bought for the shampoo bar, plus washing soda as the
builder. That mix has no enzymes and no anti-redeposition polymer. It
is a trial, not a copy of Tide. It can clean ordinary soil. Used alone,
it is the wrong tool for a greasy kitchen rag.

Softener stays vinegar in the rinse, and wool dryer balls. Not
glycerin. Not Epsom. Not baking soda.
[fabric-softener.md](fabric-softener.md) already says this. Do not put
vinegar in the same cycle as washing soda.

Scent stays the weighed concentrates in
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md). Do not pour essential oil into
the powder jar.

Stripping is occasional. Only after clothes smell sour or feel oily.
Not every week. Use unscented detergent, or the powder trial, in the
hottest water the fabric allows. Extra rinses. Run the machine’s clean
cycle if the drum is filmy. Do not mix bleach with vinegar or with
ammonia.

SLSa, SCI, and washing soda are already on the cart. These trials are
not new purchases. Borax, Fels-Naptha, and dish soap stay out of the
five trials. Those five are after “A wash that stays clean.”

## Default

Buy a powder detergent that is fragrance-free. Use the smallest scoop
on that box, then adjust. A 2× concentrate and a 6× concentrate are not
the same spoon. Machine size changes it too. There is no universal
2 tablespoons.

Then scent it from this cart. The dose is the one in
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md): 0.15–0.30 g of a Tide-shaped or
Gain-shaped concentrate per 30 g unscented portion, after the powder is
in the water. A few drops in the rinse vinegar is the other place. Not
20–30 drops of neat oil in a gallon of soap gel.

Dryer-ball doses stay in that file: 6–8 drops, no lemon on a hot ball.

Foca, Arm & Hammer, and Tide powder are names people gave. They are
examples, not endorsements, and not new shopping-list lines.

Vinegar stays in the rinse only. Washing soda stays in the wash. In the
same cycle they neutralize each other. Vinegar plus baking soda is not
a cleaner. They neutralize. A laundry-thread note calls that product
salt water. It is sodium acetate, not table salt. See
[fabric-softener.md](fabric-softener.md). (Reddit laundry thread.)

Arm & Hammer Super Washing Soda, 55 oz, is already on the cart. Washing
soda is sodium carbonate. Baking soda is not washing soda, and it is
not the detergent.

Borax, Fels-Naptha, and dish soap are not added to the priced goods
total. Sodium percarbonate, below, is grocery, unpriced, and optional.
It is not a goods-total change.

## Why those three stay off the everyday mix

Borax (20 Mule Team, sodium tetraborate) is off the default because this
household asked for no hormone-disrupting chemicals, and borax is
classified for reproductive toxicity in the EU. That is not a claim that
a cup of it will harm someone on laundry day. It is why it is not in the
everyday mix. A person can still run the labeled bag below.

Grated Fels-Naptha, Zote, Ivory, Sunlight, Marseille, and castile are
soaps, not detergent. In hard water, calcium and magnesium bind that
soap into a gray waxy scum. The film traps oil and dirt. Towels
stiffen, yellow, and smell sour. The smell often shows up after weeks,
not on load one. A common report is that it worked at first. Buildup
may not show on the first loads. A mix can seem to work while the
clothes get clean in spite of it. Plain water and friction already do
some of that. The paste’s reading of the same point is that leftover
commercial detergent, in the machine and in the clothes, was still
doing the cleaning. Washing soda reduces scum. It does not erase it.
(Green Llama; Reddit laundry thread.)

That scum collects in the drum, hoses, and filters of an HE washer,
where you cannot see it. (Green Llama; Reddit laundry thread.)
Undissolved powder and too many suds leave grit. (Reddit laundry
thread.) Kathryn Kellogg, 27 July 2020, says the same film can grow
mold or mildew, and that DIY soap can void a washer warranty. Check
this machine’s manual. That is her warning, not a line from a brand
page opened here.

Fels-Naptha and Zote do not dissolve in cool machine water. If a soap
test is run, dissolve the grated bar in a little very hot water first,
until the soap is gone. The one-bag test below stays the size limit.
(Reddit laundry thread.)

Many people use this kind of soap for years and report no sour smell.
Some add an extra rinse. One report puts citric acid in a homemade bar
for hard water, and the whites still went a little gray. One report
uses oxygen bleach on a dirty load. One of the “it works” reports is
not an HE machine. That does not erase the scum chemistry. The
experiment is still how you find out which machine you have, on a few
loads, not a 10-gallon batch. Continue a soap test only while the
clothes still smell clean after they have sat dry for a day. If they
sour, wash them again with the unscented powder and extra rinses, and
run the machine’s clean cycle. Do not invent a bleach-and-vinegar
strip. (Reddit laundry thread; a comment under Kellogg, 27 July 2020.)

Dish soap foams hard. The risk is a suds flood, not a secret failure to
clean. Test the suds in a bucket before any of it goes in the machine.

## Kathryn Kellogg, 27 July 2020

Kathryn Kellogg, “5 Reasons You Shouldn't Use Homemade Laundry
Detergent,” 27 July 2020. The page opened:
https://www.goingzerowaste.com/blog/why-you-should-never-make-laundry-detergent/

She used homemade soap about 8 months. Whites went yellow and dingy
around month 6. Clothes, towels, and sheets felt oily around month 7.
She ruined some clothing and a set of sheets. Sleeping on an oily
pillowcase bothered her skin. That is her report, not a medical claim.

Both soap and detergent have surfactants. She cites the American
Cleaning Institute: surfactants lower surface tension so water carries
dirt. Soap is fats or oils plus lye (sodium hydroxide). Detergent
surfactants are made in a plant, often more than one, and rinse more
readily. Kids Britannica is her further reading. This file does not
quote it.

Borax and washing soda are laundry boosters. They help a detergent.
They do not replace one. She writes from hard water in California.
There, soap is almost unable to rinse out of cloth.

Soap film inside the fibers can repel water, so towels stop absorbing,
and the film holds dirt. The clothes get dirtier.

The same film in the machine can grow mold or mildew. She says DIY soap
can void a washer warranty. Check the manual. That is not a quote from
a brand page opened here.

Cleaning, in her account, takes three kinds of energy. Chemical: soap
or detergent. Mechanical: an agitator or a washboard. Thermal: heat.
Modern detergent is built for cold, low-water machines. Soap wanted
hot water and a hard agitator. An older agitator machine can use about
45 gallons a load. Heating that water can erase the money and the
resource savings of a homemade mix. If someone hand-washes, or uses an
old agitator, soft water, and hot water, soap can work. It is not the
money-saving or lower-waste path on a modern machine.

Vinegar deodorizes. It is a poor stain remover. It is not the
detergent. For pet odor, vinegar stays in the rinse. That agrees with
[fabric-softener.md](fabric-softener.md).

Cheap ingredients are not cheap if you replace sheets, clothes, or a
washer.

If soap was already used for months, wash again with unscented
detergent and extra rinses, and run the machine clean cycle. She points
readers to ForTheLoveofClean.com for a larger gallery of strip photos.
The paste describes that water as nearly black. The page text that
opened here does not use those words.
https://fortheloveofclean.com/laundry-love/homemade-detergent/ did not
open here, so this file does not treat that URL as the gallery. Do not
invent a bleach-plus-vinegar strip. Do not mix bleach with vinegar or
with ammonia.

## What else stays out of the wash

Phthalate-cut perfume oils stay out. Phthalates are one of the solvents
people mean when they worry about fragrance and hormones. Undisclosed
“parfum” in Fels-Naptha, Zote, and ordinary scented detergent hides
whatever solvent or musk the bottle used. That is why the default powder
is fragrance-free, and why the scent is the concentrate already weighed
in [laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md).

Galaxolide is not on this cart. Some polycyclic musks are the musk people
mean when they worry about hormones. Do not add Galaxolide. Ethylene
brassylate is the musk already chosen for the wash scents.

Iso E Super stays out of the washer. Aquatic toxicity. Cade, clove, birch
tar, and peppermint stay out. Peppermint reads as toothpaste in a wash.

Optical brighteners are optional dye. They are not what makes dirt
leave. They are not the same thing as hormone disruptors, and they are
not a reason to switch to soap.

Dr. Bronner’s unscented baby castile, 32 oz, is already on the cart. It
is the small scent portion in [laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md), and
the hand-adjacent use already written there. It is not a 5-gallon soap
gel. Amazon castile is not a supplier here.

## Stains

Treat a stain before the dryer. The dryer can set it. Do not put a
stained piece in the dryer until the stain is handled.

## How much

The manufacturer’s line for that box is the baseline, then adjust. Too
much detergent leaves a film. Clothes feel greasy and dry slower. Too
little leaves soil. If the wash water is still frothy, the detergent is
still working. If it looks like spent gray dishwater, that load was short.

## Experiments

Each block is labeled. The default powder is the one that protects the
machine. These are small tests.

### 1. One dry bag

Optional. One bag only, not ten. This is about one tenth of a dry pile:
1 bar Fels-Naptha (about 5.5 oz), 1 cup washing soda (not baking soda),
1/2 cup borax.

| Part | One test bag |
|---|---|
| Grated Fels-Naptha | roughly 0.55 oz (about 16 g) |
| Washing soda | 1.5 tablespoons |
| Borax | 3/4 tablespoon |

Kitchen measures. Weigh the soap if you can. The tablespoons are for the two powders.
Label the bag: “borax, reproductive-toxicity concern, test.” Borax is in
this bag only. It is not in the everyday mix, and it is not a new line on
the priced cart. Mask on while the borax box is open. No dust cloud.

Gloves. The bar gets slippery. Grate it by hand. Do not grate it in a
food processor that also prepares food.

Store the bag dry. Fels-Naptha does not dissolve in cool machine water.
Zote is the same. When you want a load, put that one bag in a jug with
about 2 cups of very hot water and stir until the soap is gone. Use
1/2 cup of that liquid on one ordinary load. The 2 cups are the test
jug, not a vat. This one bag stays the size limit.

Stop if the machine over-foams, the gasket stays slimy, or the dry
clothes smell sour. Greasy garage clothes are a second load only if the
first load rinsed clean.

One person reported rashes and blamed the soap. That is a report, not a
finding. Do not diagnose from this file. Residue left on cloth is a
common reason skin reacts, so an extra rinse, or a second short rinse
cycle, is the first experiment before a new recipe. Fragrance-free
powder plus that extra rinse is the path that avoids both soap scum
and perfume. One person traced their reaction to polyethylene glycol.
That is one report, not a rule for this house. Skip was named in the
paste. Dreft, All Free and Clear, Nellie's, and Charlie's Soap are
names in the thread that opened. They are not shopping-list lines. The
person who asked was not in the US. Do not add those brands to the
cart. (Reddit laundry thread.) One person likes this kind of soap on
delicates. That is a preference, not a rule here. A forum claim says modern Fels-Naptha uses
a terpene instead of naphtha. That sentence is unverified. Fels is still
a soap, and it is still fragranced.

Sodium percarbonate (oxygen bleach, the Oxiclean / Napisan chemical)
stays a separate booster. Do not mix it into this bag or into a soap vat.

### 2. The full gel, later

Same chemistry, at a size this house should not make first. Do not cook
it until experiment 1 has rinsed clean three times. Do not store
10 gallons. A liquid gel that sits with ordinary water and no
preservative can grow mold and bacteria. That is a reason the dry bag,
mixed when you need a load, is safer than a 4-gallon gel. (Reddit
laundry thread.)

Forum batch, written so the size is visible:

| Part | Full batch |
|---|---|
| Grated soap | 12 oz |
| Washing soda | 1.5 cups |
| Borax | 1.5 cups |
| Water | gallons |
| Per load | 1/2 to 1 cup of that liquid |

If a later batch is cooked, the same stop signs apply: over-foam, a slimy
gasket, or sour clothes after drying. A grated bar melted into a
multi-gallon bucket is this batch. It is not the starting size.

### 3. Dish soap, bucket first

Optional. Not the household detergent.

Stir 1 teaspoon of dish soap into 1 gallon of water in a bucket. If that
foams over the bucket, it does not go in the washer. If the suds
collapse, one teaspoon in one load is the test, then stop.

Dawn is not the household detergent. The bucket decides whether one
teaspoon goes into one load.

### Cart pinch, a few loads

Optional. Not a substitute for a formulated HE detergent. SCI and SLSa
are on the cart for the shampoo bar. A pinch here is a few-load test,
not a new job for those jars.

Mask on. No dust clouds.

| Part | One load, weighed |
|---|---|
| Arm & Hammer Super Washing Soda | 15 g (about 1 tablespoon) |
| SCI fine powder | 1 g |

SCI is the lower-foam of the two. SLSa is the fluff in the shampoo bar.
If the pinch is SLSa instead, keep it to 1 g. Dissolve the powder in warm
water before the clothes go in. The load stays unscented until that
liquid is clear. Scent, if any, comes after that, from
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md), at that file’s dose.

Stop if the machine over-foams, or if the clothes smell sour after
drying. The dish brick and its sodium coco sulfate stay at the sink.

### Oxygen bleach, separate

Grocery, unpriced, optional. Not on the priced cart.

Sodium percarbonate, about a quarter of the scoop in that box, for
towels, washcloths, and sheets. Whites can take it too. Do not use it on
dark silks. Do not mix it into the perfume, or into the soap bag. It is
not a hormone claim. It goes in the wash, not in the vinegar rinse.

Ordinary chlorine bleach is only for a white whose label allows it. Do
not mix chlorine bleach with vinegar or with ammonia.

### If a soap gel already soured the clothes

Run the machine’s clean cycles before you blame the next recipe. Scum
can sit where you cannot see it. Wash the clothes again with the
unscented powder and extra rinses. Do not invent a bleach-and-vinegar
strip. Do not put vinegar in the wash with washing soda while you do
that. Vinegar is the rinse. Washing soda is the wash.

### Not the first batch

Heated castile gel: 6 cups water, 1 cup washing soda, 1/2 cup baking
soda, 1/2 cup castile. Castile is soap. This is not the household
default. The soap test, if you run one, is the single bag above. Amazon
castile is not a supplier here.

Laundry sheets: the microplastic and dissolvable-film worry stays an
open question. Not a purchase.

Soap berries: light soil only. Not the plan.

## A wash that stays clean

This is the everyday wash. It is not a claim that the load is
hormone-free, non-carcinogenic, or non-toxic.

Fragrance-free HE powder is the chemical energy. A paper box, if you
can find one. Use the smallest scoop that still cleans. If skin reacts,
add an extra rinse before you change the powder.

Scent, when wanted, is the weighed concentrate in
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md): 0.15–0.30 g per 30 g unscented
portion, or the dryer-ball doses already written there. No essential
oil in a soap gel. No Iso E Super in the washer. No Galaxolide. It is
not on the cart.

Vinegar only in the rinse, for odor. The dose is in
[fabric-softener.md](fabric-softener.md).

Sodium percarbonate (oxygen bleach) is an unpriced grocery booster for
towels, whites, and stains. It is not mixed into a soap vat. It is not
on the priced total. The goods total stays $1,110.09.

Washing soda already on the cart is a booster beside the powder. It is
not the detergent.

Leave off the everyday mix: borax (the EU reproductive-toxicity
classification; the labeled one-bag test remains), phthalate-cut
fragrance, optical brighteners when a box offers a version without them
(they are dyes, not the cleaner), and soap gels.

Some ethoxylated surfactants can carry 1,4-dioxane as a contaminant.
That is why a short-ingredient unscented powder is preferable to a long
perfumed liquid. That is not a claim that every detergent is
carcinogenic. Enzymes are not the harm here. Enzymes are why a modern
powder removes stains in cold water.

One powder with a published list, not added to the cart: Defunkify Free
& Clear, https://defunkify.com/products/non-toxic-powder-laundry-detergent-92-load-free-clear.
The page lists sodium sulfate, sodium carbonate, sodium citrate, LAS
alcohol ethoxylate, propoxylate, sodium metasilicate, sodium
percarbonate, sodium silicate, MGDA, polyacrylic acid, TAED, and
enzymes (protease, amylase, cellulase). It says the powder is unscented,
free of synthetic fragrance, dyes, and optical brighteners, and HE
compatible. The ethoxylated surfactant is the 1,4-dioxane note above.
The page calls the formula non-toxic. This file does not. The price on
that page is not a cart line. The page does not say the package is a
paper box.

Meliora unscented laundry powder,
https://meliorameansbetter.com/pages/ingredients, lists sodium
bicarbonate, sodium carbonate, and vegetable soap (sodium cocoate).
That is soap plus two boosters. It is not this plan, and it is not on
the cart. A second formulated powder, with a full ingredient list on a
brand page, was not opened, so no second name is stored here.

## Five wash trials

The cart powder is a few-load experiment. If three ordinary loads stay
clean and odorless after they have sat dry for a day, it can be
repeated. If they sour, go back to the commercial powder. Keep the cart
scent and the vinegar rinse. Kitchen rags stay on trial 4 even when
ordinary clothes use the commercial powder.

Borax, Fels-Naptha, and dish soap are not in these five. SLSa, SCI, and
washing soda are already on the cart.

### 1. Ordinary load, commercial control

Fragrance-free HE powder. The smallest scoop. An extra rinse. No scent.

After the clothes have sat dry for a day, they should smell like
nothing. They should not feel oily. If they smell of leftover perfume,
or feel coated, the scoop was too big or the extra rinse was skipped.
This load is the control.

### 2. Ordinary load, cart powder

One load only. Use the weighed cart pinch: 15 g washing soda and 1 g
SLSa. Mask on. No dust cloud. Dissolve both in hot water before they
go in the drum. No borax. No grated soap. A teaspoon of SLSa is more
than 1 g, and it is the fluff that over-foams, so weigh it.

Vinegar only in the rinse compartment, 1/4 cup. Not in the wash with
the washing soda. Do not add a second spoon on top of this pinch.

If the machine over-foams, stop. Run an extra rinse with no powder. If
towels stop absorbing, this dose was too much surfactant, or it did
not rinse.

SCI can replace the 1 g of SLSa in a second single load if SLSa foams
too much. SCI is the milder powder. Do not use both in one load.

After a day dry, the load should smell clean, not sour. Towels should
still take up water. If they sour, stop this powder.

### 3. Scented ordinary load

The same as trial 2, or the commercial powder, plus one scent from
[laundry-scents.md](laundry-scents.md). 0.15–0.30 g of concentrate per
30 g of a scooped portion. Or 6–8 drops of the dryer-ball concentrate
on the balls. No lemon on a hot ball. One scent at a time. Do not pour
essential oil into the powder jar.

After a day dry, you should smell that one scent, not a sour note. If
it sours, or the machine over-foams, stop and return to the unscented
control.

### 4. Kitchen rags and greasy loads

Separate from clothes and towels. Hottest water the rags allow.
Washing soda, 30 g (about 2 tablespoons). Sodium percarbonate, about 1
tablespoon. Grocery, unpriced, not on the goods total. SLSa, 2 g,
weighed and dissolved first. No vinegar in that wash. No softener. No
borax. If that foams over, drop the next rag load to 1 g SLSa.

Vinegar, 1/4 cup, in the rinse only if the rags do not smell sour.
Hang them, or dry them hot.

Do not use this on wool, silk, dark delicates, or anything labeled
cold wash. Percarbonate can fade dyes.

After a day dry, the rags should not smell greasy or sour. If they
sour, stop. Do not answer that with a bigger soap dose.

### 5. Occasional strip, not a habit

Only if a load smells sour or feels oily after drying. One wash with
the fragrance-free powder, or with trial 2 at the same small dose.
Extra rinse. No softener. No scent. Repeat once if the water from a
hand-rinse is still cloudy. Then run the machine’s clean cycle. Stop
after two tries. If it is still oily, the film needs a real detergent,
not a bigger soap dose.

After a day dry, the cloth should smell like nothing and should no
longer feel oily. If the second try is still oily, stop.
